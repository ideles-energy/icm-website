The Netherlands

The role of battery storage

Battery energy storage systems (BESS) store electricity when supply is ample and discharge it when supply is tight. In doing so, they help balance peaks in supply and demand, provide frequency and voltage regulation, and reduce the curtailment of renewable generation. Because a BESS can be deployed faster than new transmission lines, it can also serve as an interim solution where grid capacity is constrained. McKinsey expects the global BESS market to reach USD 120–150 billion by 2030.

Source: McKinsey & Company, Powering the future: Strategies for battery energy storage developers (2026)

Almere, Flevoland

A distribution centre on a 55 kW connection

55 kW → 400 kW

Prologis opened a 23,000 m² distribution centre in Almere on a site where the grid operator can provide only 55 kW of transport capacity until at least 2035. A combination of rooftop solar, battery storage and smart energy management raises the power available on site to approximately 400 kW. A gas generator operates only when additional supply is required.

Source: Het Financieele Dagblad

Cromvoirt, Noord-Brabant

125 holiday homes on a 41 kW connection

125 homes · 41 kW

At holiday park Landgoed De Zandleij in Cromvoirt, 125 holiday homes share a grid connection of 41 kW. The park charges its batteries overnight, when local electricity demand is low, and draws on the stored energy during the day.
